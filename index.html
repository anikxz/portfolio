Research Statement

My research bridges anomaly detection, deep learning, and large language model evaluation to address open problems in AI-driven cybersecurity. Drawing on my experience developing CNN-LSTM architectures for sequential anomaly detection and unsupervised methods — including Isolation Forest and One-Class SVM — for novel pattern discovery, I am particularly interested in extending these techniques to network intrusion detection, zero-day threat identification, and adversarially robust security systems.

My work evaluating LLM capabilities and limitations positions me to investigate the emerging intersection of foundation model security — specifically prompt injection vulnerabilities, LLM-assisted vulnerability detection, and the robustness of AI-driven threat analysis pipelines under adversarial conditions.

I aim to pursue PhD research that advances trustworthy, explainable, and attack-resistant AI systems for real-world cybersecurity deployment, targeting publication venues such as IEEE S&P, USENIX Security, CCS, and IEEE Transactions on Information Forensics and Security (TIFS).
